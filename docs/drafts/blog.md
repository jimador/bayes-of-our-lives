# Jev and Embabel — Bayes of Our Lives

Suppose we have a day in Miami. The museum is the easy part. The less glamorous questions arrive at seven in the morning: where are we starting, are we getting coffee, who is coming, and will lunch happen before someone decides the museum gift shop counts as food?

A map can find places. It does not know how to put a day together for this group. That is what **Bayes**, our small city outing planner, asks [Embabel Agent](https://github.com/embabel/embabel-agent) to do. The agent collects the day's requirements, researches places and routes, builds timed alternatives, then asks a decision engine a bounded question about the whole proposed outing. The engine can be local Java rules, TypeSafe Jev, or OpenAI `gpt-4.1`. We change the decision service; we do not rebuild the agent's work for each model.

[Tripper](https://github.com/embabel/tripper) gave us the shape of the journey: brief, ideas, researched points of interest, proposed plan, review, final plan. Bayes adapts those stages for one city outing. The request includes a date and time window, starting location, meals and coffee, party size and children, interests and pace, and optional access or dietary constraints. If a seven o'clock start says nothing about breakfast or coffee, the first result is a question. Place research does not begin until that answer is settled.

Bayes captures the confirmed interests and pace in [Dice](https://github.com/embabel/dice), then retrieves and validates those two facts before planning. In this version, that memory lasts **for this outing only**. It is not a stored history of previous trips. Today's meal and access requirements come from today's request; the remembered preference cannot waive them. Exact dietary or allergy notes do not enter public map searches or remote model prompts. They set a flag telling Bayes to ask for direct confirmation at a food stop.

The research is deliberately ordinary. Open-Meteo geocodes the city. Nominatim finds mapped sights and, when needed, the starting location. Photon supplies nearby mapped café and restaurant candidates. OSRM gives base driving estimates. Each candidate retains its OpenStreetMap source. Bayes reads any mapped hours, access, cuisine, or admission tags it gets, but absent tags stay unknown. Its opening-hours parser handles a conservative subset of the OSM format. A mapped Tuesday closure can invalidate Tuesday's option. A mapped opening time still needs a current check; the tag could be stale. None of these services establishes live traffic, allergy safety, or a reservation.

The planner makes two alternatives when it has two sight candidates. It adds requested food stops, assigns illustrative dwell times, and checks meal windows and the outing's finish time. A missing café becomes “Venue needed” and an open question, not a made-up breakfast. An inaccessible venue marked `wheelchair=no` cannot satisfy a step-free requirement. Unknown access stays unresolved. This is a modest planner, but the result is an itinerary with stops, times, sources, and questions to settle, rather than one ranked place.

The decision boundary is where the experiment gets interesting. The same `BayesAgent.decideItinerary` action builds an Embabel `DecisionSpec` with a proposition (“is any alternative fully sourced?”), a choice (A, B, or `research`), and a rating (`strong`, `possible`, or `weak`). It also makes a separate typed classification request for the selected itinerary shape. The input contains the confirmed Dice fields, the day's requirements, alternative statuses, timings, sources, and unresolved questions. The application checks hard constraints before it presents a plan. A model cannot turn a tagged closure into an open museum by sounding confident.

```java
var service = services.forItineraryEngine(profile.requirements().engine());
var answer = service.ask(DecisionRequest.of(input, builder));
var classification = service.classify(
    ClassificationRequest.of(input + "\nSelected itinerary type: " + type, shape));
```

That is the small configuration change we wanted to make visible. Embabel Agent still gathers and carries the facts through its actions. Dice still supplies the accepted preferences. Jev, an LLM, or our local Java implementation answers the same typed question. The result can select a tentative alternative or ask for more research. Bayes currently **reports** the remaining research; it does not automatically perform another search loop after a `research` choice.

We ran a bounded check on October 12, 2026, with synthetic, nonpersonal inputs: Miami, a downtown start, Tuesday October 13 from 7am to 2pm, breakfast and lunch, two adults, art, and a steady pace. The live lookup built breakfast–sight–lunch alternatives. One used Pérez Art Museum Miami; its mapped hours marked Tuesday closed, so Bayes invalidated it. The other used Cafe Con Leche, Patricia & Phillip Frost Art Museum, and El Cartel Colombian Restaurant. It remained unresolved because the current hours and admission needed confirmation, as did traffic. Local Java rules selected that tentative alternative. Jev and `gpt-4.1` both chose `research`. None of the three called it a confirmed day out.

Those three requests each made one decision call and one classification call. The observed decision-call times were about 8 ms locally, 401 ms for Jev, and 1,939 ms for `gpt-4.1`; classification calls were about 0.1, 175, and 983 ms respectively. There was one run per engine, no warmup, and no transport or typed-response failure in those three calls. Dice context IDs and live provider results changed between requests, so the decision inputs were not byte-for-byte identical. Remote timings include client, network, provider work, and parsing. The whole requests also include research and agent overhead. These numbers show what happened in this run; they do not establish which engine is faster in general. The [run notes](../../results/live-itinerary-check-2026-10-12.md) include the inputs, hashes, and output limits.

The interesting result is not a perfect Miami itinerary. We do not have one. It is that the agent can expose where a day stops being known. Agent handles the work, Dice carries a relevant preference into the decision, and a typed answer can choose a plan or say that more homework is due. Seven o'clock breakfast deserves at least that much honesty.
